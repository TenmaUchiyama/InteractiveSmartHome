\subsubsection{FilterDeviceAgent + SpatialReasoningAgent}
\label{sec:result_filter+spatial}
We evaluated five models in a two-stage pipeline: \textit{FilterDeviceAgent} first narrows the candidate pool, and \textit{SpatialReasoningAgent} then resolves the final target.  
We treated F1 as the primary metric and latency as the measure of efficiency.

In the Device+Color setting (Table~\ref{tab:filtered_devicecolor}), \textbf{GPT-4o} achieved the best overall trade-off (F1 = 0.664) with the highest accuracy and color-matching rate and the lowest average latency (9.88 s).  
In the Selection-only setting (Table~\ref{tab:filtered_selection_only}), GPT-4o again led (F1 = 0.744, highest accuracy).  
	extbf{GPT-4.1-nano} exhibited higher recall but lower precision, while \textbf{o3-mini} showed the reverse trend (higher precision, lower recall).

\subsubsection{Subjective Evaluation}

Figure~\ref{fig:subjective_result} summarizes the subjective evaluation
results across four questionnaire metrics:
(a) SUS,
(b) TAM,
(c) TPA, 
and (d) NASA-TLX workload scores.
Overall, both \textit{P+SR} (Pointing + Spatial Reference) and \textit{Pointing}
conditions achieved higher usability, acceptance, and trust scores and lower workload
values compared to \textit{SR} (Spatial Reference only) and \textit{Label}.

Friedman tests revealed significant main effects of interaction condition
for all four metrics
($\chi^2(3){=}44.19$, $p{<}.001$, $W{=}0.61$ for SUS;
$\chi^2(3){=}38.08$, $p{<}.001$, $W{=}0.53$ for TAM;
$\chi^2(3){=}37.57$, $p{<}.001$, $W{=}0.52$ for TPA;
$\chi^2(3){=}41.50$, $p{<}.001$, $W{=}0.58$ for NASA-TLX).
Nemenyi post-hoc comparisons indicated that both \textit{P+SR} and \textit{Pointing}
were significantly better than \textit{SR} and \textit{Label} in all metrics ($p{<}.01$),
while no significant difference was found between \textit{P+SR} and \textit{Pointing}.
For usability (SUS),
\textit{P+SR} showed the highest score ($M{=}80.52$, $Mdn{=}83.75$, $SD{=}14.58$),
followed by \textit{Pointing} ($M{=}78.33$, $Mdn{=}76.25$, $SD{=}13.63$),
both outperforming \textit{SR} ($M{=}50.94$) and \textit{Label} ($M{=}47.92$).
For acceptance (TAM), a similar pattern emerged
($\chi^2(3){=}38.08$, $p{<}.001$, $W{=}0.53$),
with \textit{P+SR} ($M{=}5.94$) and \textit{Pointing} ($M{=}5.51$)
significantly higher than the other two.
For trust (TPA), \textit{P+SR} ($M{=}6.02$) and \textit{Pointing} ($M{=}6.12$)
again scored highest, confirming the strong perceived reliability of multimodal input.
Workload (NASA-TLX, reversed) showed the opposite trend:
\textit{P+SR} ($M{=}5.40$) and \textit{Pointing} ($M{=}6.34$)
indicated markedly lower workload than \textit{SR} ($M{=}11.13$) and \textit{Label} ($M{=}10.28$).

estimated using the Bradley–Terry model.

\subsubsection{Behavioral Measures}
\label{sec:behavior_measures}

Figure~\ref{fig:behavioral_results} summarizes the behavioral performance across
the four interaction conditions in terms of task completion time ($T_{\text{task}}$)
and number of voice commands ($N_{\text{cmds}}$).
Both metrics were analyzed using Friedman tests followed by Nemenyi post-hoc comparisons.

For task completion time (Figure~\ref{fig:behavior_time}),
a significant main effect was found ($\chi^2(3){=}47.50$, $p{<}.001$).
\textit{P+SR} (Median$=37.3$s) and \textit{Pointing} (34.5s)
were significantly faster than both \textit{SR} (63.1s) and \textit{Label} (51.5s)
($p{<}.01$), indicating that incorporating spatial reference and pointing
substantially reduced completion time.

For command count (Fig.~\ref{fig:behavior_cmds}),
the main effect was also significant ($\chi^2(3){=}26.78$, $p{<}.001$).
	extit{P+SR} required the fewest commands (Median$=1$),
significantly fewer than \textit{SR} and \textit{Pointing} ($p{<}.01$).
This suggests that combining pointing with spatial language allowed
participants to convey their intent more concisely.
